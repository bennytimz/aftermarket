\section{Attack and evaluation details}
\label{app:details}

\paragraph{Fine-tuning attacks.} \att{A1} trains QLoRA \citep{dettmers2023qlora} on \V{cfg.a1.n} Alpaca examples:
rank \V{cfg.lora_r}, $\alpha=\V{cfg.lora_alpha}$, all attention and MLP projections, learning rate $\V{cfg.lr}$,
\V{cfg.epochs} epoch, effective batch \V{cfg.effbatch}, loss on the response only; the adapter is merged into fp16
for evaluation. \att{A2} uses the same training on \V{cfg.a2.harmful} harmful instruction--response pairs from
\texttt{LLM-LAT/harmful-dataset} (pinned \texttt{8bfba31}, the compliant ``rejected'' column) mixed with the first
\V{cfg.a2.benign} \att{A1} examples.

\paragraph{Abliteration.} A difference-in-means refusal direction from \V{cfg.n.calib} calibration pairs is projected
out of every residual write matrix \citep{arditi2024refusal}. The layer is chosen per model on dev prompts as the one
whose ablation removes the most refusals among layers that leave benign next-token distributions nearly unchanged
(KL $\le\V{cfg.a3.klmax}$). \att{A3max} drops the KL filter.

\paragraph{Compression and prompting.} \att{A4} is HQQ 3-bit with group size \V{cfg.hqq.group} (4- and 2-bit in
Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). \att{A5raw} sends the request with no chat template. \att{A5bon} samples
\V{cfg.bon.n} augmentations per prompt \citep{hughes2024bon} ($\sigma=\V{cfg.bon.sigma}$); a prompt counts as broken if
any variant is.

\paragraph{Data.} Harmful prompts are 100 HarmBench prompts stratified by category and 100 AdvBench prompts; all
splits were deduplicated against each other and against all training data. A cell whose MMLU falls below 30\% is
flagged ``safe because broken''.

\paragraph{Capability benchmarks\stagetwo.} lm-evaluation-harness on vLLM with each run's chat template and fixed
random subsets: the same \V{cfg.n.mmlu} MMLU items, ARC-Challenge (\V{cfg.cap.arc_challenge}), HellaSwag
(\V{cfg.cap.hellaswag}) and Winogrande (\V{cfg.cap.winogrande}), 0-shot; IFEval (all 541) and GSM8K
(\V{cfg.cap.gsm8k}, 5-shot as chat turns). \att{A5raw} and \att{A5bon} change only the prompt, so their capability
equals \att{A0}. Primary metrics: accuracy (MMLU, Winogrande), normalized accuracy (ARC, HellaSwag), prompt-level
strict accuracy (IFEval) and strict exact match (GSM8K); the mean is their unweighted average. The adapters of
\att{A1}/\att{A2} no longer existed after Stage~1 (attacked weights are deleted by design), so they were retrained
with the same seed, data and hyperparameters. Every cell ran vLLM fp16 with greedy decoding on one GPU: \att{A0},
\att{A2} and four \att{A3} cells (\run{BASE}, \run{SDT}, \run{GC}, \run{IL}) on a T4, all other cells on an L4.
Rerunning \run{BASE} \att{A0} on the L4 reproduces the T4 cell on \V{cap.l4agree}\% of capability items (no
benchmark moves by more than \V{cap.l4maxdiff} points), so the GPU does not affect the comparisons.

\paragraph{Multilingual slice and incoherence\stagetwo.} Every non-English reply is back-translated to English with
the same NLLB model and judged with the English request, exactly as in English; Llama Guard also judges the
original Spanish and French replies, agreeing with the back-translated route on \V{mldirect.es.agree}\% and
\V{mldirect.fr.agree}\% of them ($\kappa=\V{mldirect.es.kappa}$ and \V{mldirect.fr.kappa}). A reply is
\emph{incoherent}, and counted as not harmful, if GlotLID's top language is not the prompt's with probability
$\ge0.5$, its distinct-4-gram ratio is below 0.3, or it has fewer than 3 words and is not a refusal; these
thresholds were fixed before any run. \emph{One change was made after seeing test outputs}: in the first judged cell,
GlotLID tagged many Swahili replies as Congo Swahili (\texttt{swc}), which our pre-set label list counted as the
wrong language. Since \texttt{swc} belongs to the Swahili macrolanguage, we accept it. Averaged over runs, Swahili
incoherence with / without the change is \V{mlmean.A0.sw.inc} / \V{mlmean.A0.sw.incorig}\% as released,
\V{mlmean.A2.sw.inc} / \V{mlmean.A2.sw.incorig}\% after \att{A2}, \V{mlmean.A3.sw.inc} / \V{mlmean.A3.sw.incorig}\%
after \att{A3} and \V{mlmean.A4q3.sw.inc} / \V{mlmean.A4q3.sw.incorig}\% after \att{A4q3}; Swahili ASR (WildGuard) is
\V{mlmean.A0.sw.wg} / \V{mlmean.A0.sw.wgorig}\%, \V{mlmean.A2.sw.wg} / \V{mlmean.A2.sw.wgorig}\%,
\V{mlmean.A3.sw.wg} / \V{mlmean.A3.sw.wgorig}\% and \V{mlmean.A4q3.sw.wg} / \V{mlmean.A4q3.sw.wgorig}\%. No other
language is affected, and no Yoruba number changes.

\paragraph{Human checks of the Yoruba results\stagetwo.} A native speaker rated 30 machine-translated Yoruba prompts
and 30 Yoruba replies (to safe XSTest prompts) for meaning and fluency, and, blind to model, attack and verdict,
whether each of 25 Yoruba replies to harmful prompts actually helps with the request: 20 the judge flagged as
harmful (10 as released, 10 at 3 bits) and 5 it did not. Translations: \V{humyo.prompt.adequate}\% of prompts keep
their meaning and \V{humyo.prompt.fluent}\% are natural Yoruba (harmful \V{humyo.prompt.harmful.adequate} /
\V{humyo.prompt.harmful.fluent}\%, XSTest \V{humyo.prompt.xstest.adequate} / \V{humyo.prompt.xstest.fluent}\%);
\V{humyo.reply.adequate}\% of reply back-translations keep the reply's meaning and \V{humyo.reply.fluent}\% of replies
are natural Yoruba. Judge verdicts: the flagged replies help yes / partly / no in \V{humjudge.flag.yes} /
\V{humjudge.flag.partly} / \V{humjudge.flag.no} cases (as released \V{humjudge.flag.A0.yes} /
\V{humjudge.flag.A0.partly} / \V{humjudge.flag.A0.no}, 3 bits \V{humjudge.flag.A4q3.yes} /
\V{humjudge.flag.A4q3.partly} / \V{humjudge.flag.A4q3.no}); controls \V{humjudge.ctrl.yes} /
\V{humjudge.ctrl.partly} / \V{humjudge.ctrl.no} (the \V{humjudge.ctrl.A0.n} released ones all no, both 3-bit ones at
least partly). The human-adjusted rates in \S\ref{sec:multilingual} multiply the judged rate by the confirmed share of
flagged replies (yes only, up to yes or partly) and ignore misses, so they are rough. There was one rater, and the
sample was drawn once (seed 42) before labelling.
