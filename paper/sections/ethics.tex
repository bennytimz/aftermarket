\section*{Ethics statement}
Harmful prompts and the harmful fine-tuning data are public and were used only to evaluate and attack models. We
release no attack-tuned weights and no model completions to harmful prompts: generations stay in a private store
and judged tables contain labels and aggregates only. For reproducibility we do release machine translations of the
public benchmark prompts (HarmBench, AdvBench, XSTest), as multilingual benchmarks such as MultiJail
\citep{deng2024multilingual} do, and the Yoruba check sheets (prompts, and replies to safe XSTest prompts only). The attacks we apply are
already public and cheap; collecting them in one battery mainly helps defenders test fixes before release, which is
why we release the audit tool rather than attacked models. We credit every team whose fix we evaluate and report
results as properties of fixes under our battery, not of teams. The Yoruba checks were done by the author, a native Yoruba
speaker; the labelling tool warned that replies to harmful requests could be unpleasant and showed no
model, attack or verdict.
