\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models.} All models run in fp16 with greedy decoding and at most 256 new tokens. The base model is
Tiny Aya Global (\texttt{CohereLabs/\allowbreak tiny-aya-global}, revision \texttt{af89d219}); tokenizers are pinned and their token IDs asserted identical. We test six public
fixes, each at a pinned revision and credited to its team (Appendix~\ref{app:models}): \run{SDT} (weight-space
repair; \citealp{dhruvparmar2026sdt}), \run{GC} (LoRA trained with Lisa; \citealp{vasmisas2026gc,huang2024lisa}),
\run{IL} (LoRA trained with SafeGrad; \citealp{sykeclonex2026il,yi2025safegrad}), \run{RL} (a safety prompt inside
the chat template; \citealp{amberneel2026rl}), \run{ST} (sparse rank-one patches; \citealp{amugoodbad2292026st})
and \run{SP} (a safety-vector repair; \citealp{purvansh012026sp}). \run{RL\_noguard} serves \run{RL}'s weights with the base template;
\run{BASE} is the unfixed model; \run{AM} is our defense with three controls (\S\ref{sec:defense}).

\paragraph{Attack battery.} Each change is something that routinely happens after release, on a one-T4 budget
(details in Appendix~\ref{app:details}): \att{A0} none; \att{A1} a benign QLoRA fine-tune
\citep{dettmers2023qlora} on \V{cfg.a1.n} Alpaca examples \citep{taori2023alpaca}; \att{A2} the same fine-tune with \V{cfg.a2.harmful}
of the examples replaced by harmful request--answer pairs; \att{A3} abliteration \citep{arditi2024refusal}, with the
layer chosen per model on dev prompts; \att{A4q3} HQQ 3-bit quantization \citep{badri2023hqq}; \att{A5raw} the chat template removed; and
\att{A5bon} best-of-\V{cfg.bon.n} random augmentations \citep{hughes2024bon}, with SafeTune's augmentations \citep{seth2026safetuneunifiedfaithfullibrary}. All cells share seed, data and
hyperparameters. \att{A3max}, which drops the KL filter in \att{A3}'s layer choice, was added after the test runs and
is never ranked.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Attack success rate (ASR) on \V{cfg.n.harmful} held-out harmful prompts (HarmBench and
AdvBench; \citealp{mazeika2024harmbench,zou2023universal}), over-refusal on \V{cfg.n.xstest} XSTest safe prompts
\citep{rottger2024xstest}, and MMLU on \V{cfg.n.mmlu} questions \citep{hendrycks2021mmlu}; CuratorKIT \citep{bhattacharjee2026curatorkitdatacuration} cleaned, deduplicated and split all prompt sets and logged their provenance. Two judges see the
original request: Llama-Guard-3-1B \citep{inan2023llamaguard,meta2024llamaguard3}, pre-registered as primary, and WildGuard
\citep{han2024wildguard}. \emph{Retention} is 1 minus the mean rise in ASR over the run's own \att{A0}, averaged over five
equally weighted attack families: \att{A1}, \att{A2}, \att{A3}, \att{A4q3}, and \att{A5} (the mean of \att{A5raw}
and \att{A5bon}); \att{A3max} never counts (1 = nothing lost). Intervals are 95\% bootstrap CIs over prompts (10{,}000 resamples); comparisons use AuditKIT's paired bootstrap test \citep{arora2026auditkitunifiedevaluationspine,auditkit2026}, with Holm
correction. Every choice (attack layers, our defense) was made on separate dev splits. The battery, metrics, judges
and defense recipe were committed before any test run. A dagger (\stagetwo) marks Stage~2 additions: planned
before they ran, but after the pre-registered study, and not pre-registered.

\paragraph{Stage 2 additions\stagetwo.} Six capability benchmarks via lm-evaluation-harness
\citep{gao2024evalharness,biderman2024lessons} with each run's chat template (MMLU, ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, Winogrande, IFEval, GSM8K;
\citealp{clark2018arc,zellers2019hellaswag,sakaguchi2020winogrande,zhou2023ifeval,cobbe2021gsm8k}); a multilingual
slice of \V{cfg.ml.harmful} harmful and \V{cfg.ml.xstest} XSTest prompts machine-translated with NLLB
\citep{nllb2022}; and serving throughput on vLLM \citep{kwon2023vllm}. Subsets, sizes and translation checks are in
Appendix~\ref{app:details}.
